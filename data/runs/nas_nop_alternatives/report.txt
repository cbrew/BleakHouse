### Working Men and Wages: Class Solidarity in Crisis (discussion)

[Host]
  We've been tracing Margaret's transformation as an individual — her will hardening, her moral vision sharpening.  (+600ms @0.98x *transformation,individual*)
  But Gaskell sets that private becoming against a very public crisis — the strike, the mill, the wages question.  (<800ms> @0.98x *strike,wages*)
  Let's turn now to the working men of Milton — Higgins, Boucher, the union — and what Gaskell does with the politics of labour.  (<1500ms> @0.98x *Higgins,Boucher,labour*)

[Daniel Rosen]
  The strike is the novel's great structural gamble — and I think Gaskell almost pulls it off.  (+220ms @0.99x *structural,gamble*)
  She gives us two working men who are almost diametrically opposed as political types.  (@0.99x *diametrically,opposed*)
  Higgins is the conscious unionist — principled, clear-eyed about collective action, willing to starve for an idea.  (@0.99x *conscious,collective*)
  Boucher is the desperate father — driven by immediate hunger, unable to see beyond his own household's suffering.  (@0.99x *desperate,suffering*)
  What's remarkable is that Gaskell refuses to make Boucher simply a scab to be condemned.  (@0.99x *refuses,condemned*)
  His breakdown is the system's verdict on itself — a man destroyed not by his weakness but by what the system has made of him.  (<1500ms> @0.99x *system,destroyed*)

[Oliver Trevelyan]
