I find that reading genuinely moving, and I think Oliver has identified something that the novel's admirers sometimes overlook. Virginia is not a polemical figure. Daniel Rosen (marxist_critic)
I want to be careful here, because I'm not saying Gissing doesn't feel for Virginia — he plainly does, and the passage is written with real tenderness. But the structure of the scene is worth examining precisely because it is so tender. Virginia is a woman without resources, without occupation, slowly declining — and what restores her for a quarter of an hour is sunshine and fresh air. It is hope that has been severed from any material possibility of improvement. And Gissing, I think, understands this with terrible clarity — even as he offers us the moment as a consolation.
