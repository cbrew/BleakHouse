### Wragge's Comic Apparatus: Scheming and Survival (deep_dive)

[Host]
  We've been talking about the social machinery Collins builds into this novel — law, inheritance, class — and how that machinery grinds against people with no legitimate claim to power.  (+600ms @0.98x *machinery,power*)
  Which makes it the perfect moment to turn to the character who has decided, with magnificent self-awareness, to become a machine of his own.  (@0.98x *machine*)
  Captain Wragge: swindler, schemer, theatrical impresario, and arguably the most entertaining figure in the whole novel.  (<800ms> *Wragge,entertaining*)

[Oliver Trevelyan]
  The first thing Collins does with Wragge — and it is a masterstroke — is describe him through his eyes.  [c18:p3]  (+220ms @1.02x *eyes*)
  Not his clothes, not his posture — his eyes.  [c18:p3]
  And Collins writes it like this:  [c18:p3]  (@0.98x [setup])
  > "he surveyed the scene around him with eyes of two different colors — a bilious brown eye on the lookout for employment, and a bilious green eye in a similar predicament."  [c18:p3]  (+150ms @0.93x [reading] *bilious,employment,predicament*)
  Two eyes, two different colours, both bilious, both hungry — and both, crucially, in the same predicament.  [c18:p3]  ([commentary] *bilious,predicament*)
  Collins is telling you everything about the man in a single sentence: this person is comically, constitutionally divided, and all of him is looking for a way to survive.  [c18:p3]  (<800ms> @1.02x *divided,survive*)
  And he does it with a gingham umbrella in an oilskin case, picking his steps neatly around every dirty patch — respectability as performance, down to the last fastidious footfall.  [c18:p3]  (<1500ms> @1.02x *respectability,performance*)

[Edmund Leigh]
  There is a long tradition in English comic writing — Fielding, Smollett — of the rogue who narrates his own villainy with such relish that we find ourselves utterly on his side.  (+220ms @0.94x *rogue,relish*)
  Wragge belongs to that tradition.  (@0.94x)
  But what distinguishes him — and I think Oliver is right to press on this — is that Wragge is not merely charming.  [c19:p70]  (@0.94x *charming*)
  He is systematic.  [c19:p70]  (@0.92x *systematic*)
  When he lays out his six-book system to Magdalen, there is something almost admirable in the rigour of it.  [c19:p70]  (@0.94x *rigour*)
  He understands human weakness — specifically, the weakness of the middling sort — better than any philanthropist ever could.  [c19:p70]  (<800ms> @0.94x *weakness,philanthropist*)
  He says — and I find this genuinely acute — that rich people never forgive the genteel beggar for swindling them out of a sovereign; it is servants and small clerks, to whom shillings and sixpences are of consequence, who lose money by sheer thoughtlessness. [Daniel Rosen]

  Wragge doesn't prey on the rich.  [c19:p70]  (@0.99x)

  The system he describes — districts, cases, adopted handwritings, forged testimonials — isn't chaos.  [c19:p70]  (@0.99x *districts,forged*)

[c19:p70]  (+150ms @0.93x [reading] *Waterloo,debility,bull*)

  And Wragge has catalogued them with the precision of a market researcher.  [c19:p70]  (@0.99x *catalogued,precision*)

[Oliver Trevelyan]
