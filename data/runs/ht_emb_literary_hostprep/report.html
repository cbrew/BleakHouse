James Blackstone (social_historian)
The bargain is: we will permit conditions that degrade human beings, provided they produce what we need, and we will find a language — whether it is 'progress' or 'efficiency' or 'the market' — that makes the degradation sound like necessity. Coketown's equivalent is wherever the cost of a thing we consume is being paid by a body we cannot see. The blur of smoke in that distance passage — 'a dense formless jumble, with sheets of cross light in it, that showed nothing but masses of darkness' — that is also a description of supply chains. Dickens helps us see it because he refuses to let the poetry become a consolation. The beauty of the prose is in the service of discomfort, not of pleasure.
