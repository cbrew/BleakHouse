### A Girl at the Window: Maggie's First Hunger (opening)

[Host]

  I'm joined today by three people who have been living with this novel and who disagree, productively, about almost everything it means.  (@0.98x *disagree,productively*)
  Oliver Trevelyan has narrated more classic novels than almost anyone alive — he comes to Eliot as a performer, listening for the rhythm and the comic timing built into every sentence.  (@0.98x *performer,rhythm*)

  And Dr. Daniel Rosen is a cultural historian who reads novels as anatomies of class power — for Daniel, every institution in the book, every law, every inheritance dispute, is a mechanism for keeping certain people exactly where they are.  (<800ms> @0.98x *class,power,mechanism*)

  Edmund, let's start with you — you've called the window scene 'character at its most naked.' What does Eliot actually do, on the sentence level, that makes us feel Maggie's hunger rather than simply understand it?  (<1500ms> @0.98x *feel,hunger,sentence*)

### The Law's Long Shadow: Property, Debt, and the Tulliver Collapse (deep_dive)

[Host]

  You've said he fails because the system was built to fail him.  (@0.99x *system,built*)

[Daniel Rosen]

  Yes, Tulliver is proud. Yes, he launches a disastrous lawsuit over water rights on the Ripple.

  But what kind of world is it in which a man of that class — a miller who owns a little property — has no institutional buffer at all?  (@0.99x *buffer,institutional*)
  His pride, his stubbornness — these aren't moral failings in isolation. They are symptoms of a man who has no other resource.  (@0.98x *symptoms,resource*)
  The law is not neutral in this novel. The moment Tulliver enters a legal dispute, he is already on ground that belongs to people like Wakem.  (@0.99x *Wakem,ground*)

[Edmund Leigh]
  I find that reading seductive and finally evasive.  (+220ms @0.94x *evasive*)

[Oliver Trevelyan]

[Daniel Rosen]

### Mud, Floods, and the English Midlands: Reading Eliot's Setting as Character (close_reading)

[Host]
  We've been tracing how the law and debt brought the Tulliver family to ruin — but of course, the Tullivers don't just live inside legal documents and balance sheets.  (+600ms @0.98x *law,debt*)
  They live inside a landscape — a very specific, very wet, very English one — and that landscape is what I want us to turn to now.  (@0.98x *landscape,wet*)
  Oliver, before we started recording today you told me you basically can't discuss this novel without reading it aloud — that the mud is somehow in the syntax.  (*syntax,aloud*)
  So I want to hold you to that. Give us the opening description of St. Ogg's — read it to us — and then tell us what we just heard that we'd have missed on the page.  (<800ms> @0.98x *St. Ogg's,heard*)

[Oliver Trevelyan]
  Right. I'll do it. And I should warn everyone that when I do this at parties, I clear the room.  (+220ms @1.03x *parties*)
  But you're asking me to do something Eliot practically demands — because the novel's first movement is almost purely sonic.  (@1.01x *sonic,demands*)
  She describes St. Ogg's as — and I'm going to read this carefully —  (@0.97x [setup])
  > "one of those old, old towns which impress one as a continuation and outgrowth of nature, as much as the nests of the water-birds or the weeds upon the river."  (+150ms @0.92x [reading] *continuation,nature,weeds*)
  Now listen to what she does there. She's not describing a place — she's performing its age.  ([commentary] *performing,age*)
  That phrase — 'old, old towns' — the repetition is almost incantatory. It slows the reader down physically.  (@0.99x *repetition,incantatory*)
  And then she drops you into the water-birds and the weeds — you're not reading about a town anymore, you're reading about something that grew, not something that was built.  (@1.02x *grew,built*)
  On the page that looks like a nice descriptive touch. Out loud, it feels like deep time pressing down on you.  (<1500ms> *deep time,pressing*)

[Daniel Rosen]
  That's beautiful, Oliver, and I mean that. But notice what that beautiful sentence is actually doing.  (+200ms @0.99x *actually doing*)
  St. Ogg's is ancient — it's 'a continuation of nature' — and that naturalisation is ideological work.  (@0.99x *naturalisation,ideological*)
