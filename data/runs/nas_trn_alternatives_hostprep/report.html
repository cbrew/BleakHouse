Daniel Rosen (marxist_critic)
I love what Oliver does with that passage — and I don't entirely disagree about the performance. But I think if we stop at 'gut-level betrayal' we miss something structurally important. Higgins says — and this is the sentence that stops me every time — 'Why he'd a clemmed to death, if th' Union had na helped him in his pinch.' "Why he'd a clemmed to death, if th' Union had na helped him in his pinch." 'Clemmed' — starved. That's the word. Boucher would have starved. And the Union kept him alive, which means the Union holds a debt over him — and now he's defaulted on that debt. What I want to ask is: isn't that debt structure — mutual obligation enforced under threat of survival — an exact mirror of how the mill-owners hold power over their workers? When Higgins calls him Judas, capital is, in a very real sense, speaking through a worker's mouth.

I find that reading genuinely ingenious, and quite wrong. Gaskell is not writing a structural critique of debt mechanisms — she is writing about loyalty, and what happens to a man when he betrays it.
