Steering notes

The three readings here form a genuinely productive triangle: Edmund reads Rhoda psychologically (a woman performing certainty she half-doubts), Daniel reads her structurally (a woman whose liberation is indistinguishable from her exploitation), and Oliver reads her theatrically (a woman whose ideology works because it has to, because vulnerability would destroy her). The segment works best if we resist resolving that triangle — let all three feel partially right and partially insufficient. The 'great reserve' passage and the 'personal feeling' line are the two load-bearing quotes; get them said aloud at least once each. Watch for Edmund and Daniel talking past each other on the question of inner conviction vs. structural position — that's the most generative friction in the room. Keep energy pub-conversational: these are smart people who disagree and enjoy it, not academics scoring points.

Cross-engagement targets

  • Edmund's claim that Rhoda 'protests too much' — that her certainty is performed — directly maps onto Oliver's theatrical reading but cuts against Daniel's structural one: if she's genuinely uncertain, she's tragic; if she's structurally captured, her uncertainty is beside the point.   • Daniel's reading of the 'great reserve' metaphor as capitalist logic directly contradicts Edmund's point that Rhoda is 'constructing a personal myth of purpose' — one sees ideology, the other sees psychology. Force them to decide whether those are compatible or mutually exclusive.
  • Oliver's observation that Rhoda argues from scarcity (the odd women are visible only because married women create a deficit) is a structural point that actually supports Daniel's reading more than Oliver might want to admit — flag this and see if Oliver accepts or resists the alliance.

The 'I mean' is everything. Rhoda starts with an abstraction — 'one of us again' — and then hears how hollow it sounds against a woman who nearly died of a bad marriage.
