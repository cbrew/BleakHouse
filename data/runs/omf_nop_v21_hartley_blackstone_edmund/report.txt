### The Weight of Dust: Boffin's Moral Reckoning (deep_dive)

[Host]

  We've spent time in the dust mounds and the river — the novel's great engines of death and inheritance.  (@0.98x *dust mounds,river*)

  Boffin is, on the surface, one of Dickens's great benevolent figures — warm, generous, a little comic.  (@0.98x *benevolent*)
  But the novel does something deeply unsettling with him as it proceeds.  (@0.97x *unsettling*)
  He appears to harden — to become a miser, a tyrant, a bully toward the very people he once cherished.  (@0.97x *miser,tyrant*)
  And then — in one of the novel's great reveals — we discover it was all a performance, an elaborate stratagem to protect John Rokesmith and test Bella Wilfer.  (@0.98x *performance,stratagem*)
  So the question we're asking today is: what does Dickens actually achieve with this moral reckoning — and does it entirely convince?  (<1500ms> @0.98x *moral reckoning,convince*)

[Eleanor Hartley]
  What strikes me first is the sheer structural daring of what Dickens attempts with Boffin.  (+220ms @1.01x *structural daring*)
  He essentially asks the reader to misread a character for several hundred pages — and then pulls the rug away.  (@1.01x *misread,rug*)
  The miser performance is written with such conviction — Boffin's sneering at Rokesmith, his sudden obsessive interest in the biographies of famous misers — that many readers, original readers included, were genuinely deceived.  (@1.01x *conviction,deceived*)
  There's a moment where Boffin demands that Silas Wegg read him the life of Dancer — the notorious real-life miser — and he listens with a kind of hungry relish.  (@1.01x *Dancer,relish*)
  Dickens gives us just enough of Boffin's interior glee to confuse us — is this corruption or theatre?  (@1.02x *corruption,theatre*)
  That ambiguity is the engine of the whole device.  (<800ms> @0.98x *ambiguity,engine*)
  And I think Dickens is reaching for something about what money does to the way we read people — even people we trust.  (<1500ms> *money,trust*)

[James Blackstone]
  That institutional point about reading people through money is worth dwelling on, because it has a very precise social location in this novel.  (+220ms @0.96x *social location,money*)
  Boffin inherits the Harmon dust fortune — and in mid-Victorian England, dust contracting was a serious, lucrative, and deeply unglamorous trade.  (@0.96x *dust contracting,unglamorous*)

  So Boffin's fortune is built on what society throws away.  (@0.95x *throws away*)
  When he performs miserliness — when he pretends to become old Harmon reborn — he is inhabiting the spirit of the man who accumulated that wealth through parsimony and cruelty.  (@0.96x *inhabiting,parsimony,cruelty*)
  There is something historically very pointed in Dickens's implication that great wealth, however inherited, carries the character of its original accumulator.  (@0.95x *carries,accumulator*)
  The dust does not become clean simply because it changes hands.  (<1500ms> @0.94x *dust,clean*)

[Edmund Leigh]
  That is elegantly put, though I think it risks flattening what is, at heart, a story about individual moral courage rather than social determinism.  (+260ms @0.94x *moral courage,determinism*)
  Boffin does not succumb to the corrupting force of the dust fortune — he resists it, actively and at considerable personal cost.  (@0.94x *resists,personal cost*)
  What Dickens shows us is that a man of good character — genuinely good, not sentimentally good — can handle great wealth without being devoured by it.  (@0.94x *good character,devoured*)
  And the stratagem he devises — the performance of miserliness — is an act of considerable moral imagination.  (@0.94x *stratagem,moral imagination*)
  He submits himself to being thought ill of — by Bella, by the reader, by Society — in order to secure a genuine human good.  (@0.93x *thought ill of,genuine*)
  That is not social critique — that is what the older moral tradition would call self-abnegation in service of love.  (@0.93x *self-abnegation,love*)
  Johnson, I think, would have recognised something admirable in it.  (<1500ms> @0.94x *Johnson,admirable*)

[Eleanor Hartley]
  I don't disagree with Edmund about Boffin's moral courage — but I want to push on the craft question of whether the reveal actually works.  (+220ms @1.01x *reveal,works*)

[James Blackstone]

### A Woman Remade: Bella's Long Becoming (deep_dive)
