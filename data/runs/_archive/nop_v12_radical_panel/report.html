Daniel Rosen (marxist_critic)
I don't think those readings are incompatible — but I'd say the moral language is the vehicle, not the destination. Dickens uses Biblical register because it was the emotional vocabulary his readers recognised. But the object of his wrath is thoroughly historical. The Jarndyce and Jarndyce suit is not an allegory of original sin. It is a documented portrait of how the Court of Chancery actually operated. Cases ran for decades. Families destroyed by legal fees before a judgment was ever reached. Dickens knew this. His own experience as a law clerk gave him a ground-level view of the machinery.
Caroline Woodcourt (close_reader)
What I'd add — and I think it connects both of your readings — is the sheer strangeness of Dickens' narrative voice in those opening pages. He's writing in an eternal present tense.
