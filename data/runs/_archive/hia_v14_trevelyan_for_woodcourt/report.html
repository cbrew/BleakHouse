James Blackstone (social_historian)
The Smallweeds are one of Dickens's most controlled comic inventions — but the comedy is inseparable from a genuine sociological argument. What he's describing, with deadpan precision, is the effect of a single-minded commercial culture transmitted across three generations. The grandfather goes out early into commerce, marries late, produces a lean and anxious son, who does exactly the same, who produces Bartholomew and Judith Smallweed — twins. Each generation, the narrator tells us, has discarded all amusements, discountenanced all story-books, fairy-tales, fictions, and fables — and banished all levities whatsoever. The cumulative result of this relentless pruning is what Dickens delivers as a kind of triumphant conclusion — and the word 'gratifying' does a great deal of ironic work. "Hence the gratifying fact that it has had no child born to it and that the complete little men and women whom it has produced have been observed to bear a likeness to old monkeys with something depressing on their minds." Old monkeys with something depressing on their minds — that is a sentence written for posterity. And the historical point underneath the joke is serious: this is what the moneylending and discounting trades actually did to family life in nineteenth-century London.

Strongly is an understatement — it's one of the great scenes of restraint in Victorian fiction, and it almost shouldn't work. Esther's narration throughout has been careful, modest — she qualifies everything she says about herself. James Blackstone (social_historian)
And that irony is historically precise, not merely rhetorical. In mid-Victorian England, witnesses at a coroner's inquest could be disqualified if they failed to demonstrate understanding of the Christian oath. The practical effect was to exclude precisely those people — the uneducated, the destitute — most likely to be witnesses to deaths caused by poverty and neglect. Dickens understood that the law's stated neutrality was in practice a class weapon. Jo's exclusion is not a failure of the system — it is the system working exactly as designed.
