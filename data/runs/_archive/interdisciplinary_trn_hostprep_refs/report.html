Elena Volkov
Dickens is doing in prose what Shostakovich will do in music a hundred years later: encoding institutional critique in formal structure rather than content, precisely because content is deniable and structure is not. Miss Flite's broken word 'respectable' is not a piece of political content — it is a cadential failure, and its implication is more devastating than any reformist pamphlet: the system has not just destroyed her family, it has destroyed the grammar of ordinary life. The most powerful argument in Bleak House is made by a broken syllable and a list that runs out of categories.
Key points:
  • The three passages (Boythorn, Krook, Miss Flite) are not three different things but the same formal structure repeated at three different dynamic levels — theme and variations on institutional consumption, escalating from comedic forte to hollow pianissimo to sforzando shock.
  • The Boythorn passage is not resistance to Chancery's logic but an internalisation of it: the ostinato of 'He brings actions for trespass; I bring actions for trespass' is the same recursive machine that destroys Miss Flite, just operating at a different register and celebrated rather than mourned.
  • The prose rhythm of the Miss Flite passage enacts a collapsed recapitulation — the broken hyphen on 'Ve-ry respectab—' is a cadential failure, the tonic chord approached and then erased, demonstrating structurally what the system does to the grammar of ordinary life.
  • The Krook passage is not primarily metaphor but a formal argument about language: the narrator proceeds in the syntax of legal inventory ('here is... here is...') until that archival language fails to categorise what is present, and the collapse into 'Oh, horror, he IS here!' is the moment official procedure breaks against human reality.
