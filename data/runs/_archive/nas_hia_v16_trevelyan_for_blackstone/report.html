Eleanor Hartley (literary_critic)
Oliver's absolutely right about the accumulation — and I'd push further into the architecture of that sentence. Gaskell uses anaphora — that repeated 'all... all' — to enact the very thing she's describing: memory flooding in. Each clause is another wave arriving before the previous one has subsided. And notice what triggers it — not grief directly, but likeness. The aunt's face, her tone, her gesture — the family resemblance to the dead mother. It's a brilliantly observed truth about bereavement — that it's often the indirect echo, not the direct confrontation, that finally undoes us. Gaskell was writing from experience, and it shows in every syllable.

That contrast — the mundane and the devastating — is everywhere in these passages. Take the passage about Thornton — which is, I think, one of the most quietly heartbreaking things in the novel. Margaret has called Milton the place where she suffered so much — and Gaskell shows us what that same place means to Thornton. The asymmetry is almost unbearable. Eleanor Hartley (literary_critic)
What strikes me immediately about these chapters is that Gaskell gives Margaret two completely different mirrors to hold up to Milton. One is social and satirical — her encounter with the Thorntons, with Mrs. Thornton's formidable civic pride. The other is tragic and intimate — her visits to Bessy Higgins, a mill worker quite literally dying from her labour. Gaskell holds those two registers in the same chapter, and that structural audacity is what makes the novel so extraordinary. Consider Margaret's judgement of Thornton — it's so revealing of her formation as a person. She tells her father: "Everything else revolted me, from its hardness; but he spoke about himself so simply—with so little of the pretence that makes the vulgarity of shop-people, and with such tender respect for his mother." That word 'revolted' is doing enormous work — she's nineteen, she's well-bred, she's instinctively class-conscious, and yet she can see past that the moment genuine feeling appears.
