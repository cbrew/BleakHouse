Daniel Rosen (marxist_critic)
And what the fog conceals, above everything else, is the machinery of Chancery. The Court of Chancery in the 1850s was not merely slow — it was a system designed to transfer wealth upward and frustration downward. Dickens understood that — not perfectly, not in a worked-out theoretical way, but viscerally, from watching real people destroyed by it. There's a moment later in the novel where Allan Woodcourt is guiding the dying Jo through the Lord's Prayer. "It is close at hand. Hallowed be thy name!" The light that is 'close at hand' is death. That is what the system offers him.
