Oliver Trevelyan (performer_and_wit)
With pleasure — and I'd ask you to just listen to what the language does before you think about what it means. "Fog everywhere. Fog up the river, where it flows among green aits and meadows; fog down the river, where it rolls defiled among the tiers of shipping and the waterside pollutions of a great (and dirty) city." You hear what he's done there? The word 'fog' hits you eleven times in that opening sequence — eleven times before you've been told a single name or a single fact. He's not describing the fog — he's enacting it. The language itself becomes thick and repetitive and inescapable, just as the fog is. And then he does something quite extraordinary — he descends from the grand to the pitiable. He moves from rivers and marshes all the way down to the 'shivering little 'prentice boy on deck' — pinched toes and fingers. That's a novelist who knows exactly what he's doing — the cosmic and the intimate in the same breath.

Daniel Rosen (marxist_critic)
I want to hold onto that descent Oliver just described — because it's precisely where the fog stops being universal. The skipper is in his close cabin, wrathful, pipe in hand. The apprentice boy is on deck, shivering. The fog is in both their lives, yes — but it is not doing the same thing to both of them. In our conversation before this, I used the word 'manufactured' — and I want to explain what I mean by that. This fog doesn't descend from nowhere. Look at the companion passage — 'smoke lowering down from chimney-pots, making a soft black drizzle, with flakes of soot in it as big as full-grown snowflakes.' That is industrial combustion rendered as weather. The fog is what the city produces — and what the city then forces the poorest to breathe. So I would say to Oliver, with great respect: the spell is real, the artistry is extraordinary — but the novel is also being documentary. Dickens is not conjuring an atmosphere — he is recording a condition.

Edmund Leigh (traditionalist_critic)
I find both readings illuminating, and both, I think, somewhat incomplete. What strikes me about that London passage is not its social diagnosis but its moral atmosphere. Dickens opens the novel with the sentence 'Implacable November weather' — and that word, implacable, is doing a great deal of work. Implacable means unyielding, unappeasable — it is the word for a force that will not be moved by human appeal. Dickens is not asking us where the fog came from. He is asking what we do in its presence — how we bear it, whether we succumb to it, whether we retain our moral bearings inside it. That is the question the whole novel is going to ask of each of its characters.

Daniel Rosen (marxist_critic)
The tonal freedom is real — but I think the Megalosaurus is also making a specific argument about time. This is supposedly the most advanced city in the world, the capital of empire — and Dickens is saying: look again. The mud and the fog and the compound interest of filth accumulating at street corners — this is not progress. 'Accumulating at compound interest' — he actually uses that phrase about the mud. That is a Victorian bourgeois virtue — thrift, accumulation — applied to filth. Dickens is suggesting that the logic of accumulation and the logic of squalor are the same logic.

Daniel Rosen (marxist_critic)
Exactly — and the Vholes passage is where Dickens gets most precise about how the mechanism works. Listen to Mr. Kenge's defence of his colleague. "Now you cannot afford — I will say, the social system cannot afford — to lose an order of men like Mr. Vholes." Notice the sleight of hand: 'you cannot afford' becomes 'the social system cannot afford' in the same breath. The individual's complaint is dissolved into a structural argument for the status quo. And then there's the parliamentary inquiry — which runs to question number five hundred and seventeen thousand eight hundred and sixty-nine. Every answer confirms delay, expense, vexation — and yet the inquiry collapses the moment someone says Vholes is, in the profession, a most respectable man. The fog doesn't hide Vholes accidentally — respectability is the fog, deliberately deployed.
