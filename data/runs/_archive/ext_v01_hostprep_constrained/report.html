Eleanor Hartley (literary_critic)
Yes — and I want to be precise about this, because it's easy to say 'the prose is atmospheric' and leave it there. What Dickens is doing in this passage is something far more calculated. The passage describes a man who has died alone — a man called Nemo, Nobody — and Dickens describes his anonymity through the shape of the sentence itself. "lies there with no more track behind him that any one can trace than a deserted infant." That phrase — 'no more track than a deserted infant' — it circles back on itself. The sentence enacts the erasure it's describing — it trails off exactly where the man's life trails off. You don't receive the information so much as undergo the experience of it — which is exactly what the fog does to the reader from the very first page.

I want to resist, mildly, the word 'calculated' — because Dickens was writing in monthly instalments under deadline pressure, and I think we should be careful about over-attributing architectural intention. James Blackstone (social_historian)
I don't disagree that it's a brilliant piece of construction. But I think calling it a masterstroke of narrative architecture risks aestheticising something that is, in the novel's own terms, a depiction of epistemic violence. What Dickens is showing us is a society in which knowledge is hoarded — where power operates precisely by controlling what can be known and by whom. Tulkinghorn is not a character who withholds for dramatic effect. He is a system — the legal system, the class system, the system of property and inheritance — that depends on ordinary people not knowing what is being done to them. And when Dickens writes, in that same chapter forty-one, that Tulkinghorn stops Lady Dedlock at the door with the words — "have the goodness to hear me, or before you reach the staircase I shall ring the alarm-bell and rouse the house" — that's not a villain's line. That is the voice of an institution telling a woman that her only choices are compliance or public destruction. The form replicates the content, yes — but the content is coercion, not craft.
