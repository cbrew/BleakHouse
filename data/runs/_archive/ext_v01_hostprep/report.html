Eleanor Hartley (literary_critic)
You're both reading the content of that passage — and I think the form is doing something that changes both your arguments. Look at the syntax. The coffin stands ready — but the man has no track. Dickens yokes that active, waiting object — the coffin — against a life that leaves no trace. The coffin is more present, more materially solid, than the man who will go inside it. That inversion — object outlasting person — is the formal thesis of the whole novel. Chancery, Chesney Wold, the lawsuits — all of these institutions persist; the people inside them dissolve.
