Eleanor Hartley (literary_critic)
It does — and I think that's the first thing to say about this novel: it refuses to make things easy. Dickens gives us two narrators — one omniscient, cold, sardonic, speaking in present tense; the other Esther Summerson, first-person, warm, uncertain, looking back. And the effect of that double structure is that we are always slightly off-balance — always reading two versions of the same world simultaneously. What I find remarkable is that Esther's narration achieves something technically very difficult: she must seem modest and self-deprecating while actually being the novel's moral centre. And Dickens threads that needle brilliantly — by loading her narration with dramatic irony. There is a passage late in the novel — in Chapter 59 — that shows this at its most devastating. Esther kneels at the gate, lifts the head of a fallen woman, and Dickens writes: "And it was my mother, cold and dead." Seven words. The entire architecture of the novel — the secrets, the shame, the long separation — collapses into seven words.

Eleanor Hartley (literary_critic)
Which brings me to Tulkinghorn — because the novel holds Jenny's world and Tulkinghorn's world in the same frame, and the contrast is merciless. There's a passage in Chapter 41 where Tulkinghorn lays out his case to Lady Dedlock with a coldness that is genuinely chilling. He says, in what Dickens calls his "jog-trot style": "If I inflicted this shock upon him to-morrow morning, how could the immediate change in him be accounted for? What could have caused it?" He is not interested in Lady Dedlock's suffering — she is, in his own words, someone he "cannot at all consider in this business." His only concern is how the revelation looks — how it can be managed, hushed up, contained. He is the legal system made flesh — and the legal system, in this novel, is indifferent to human pain.

One ought to pause on what Jarndyce actually does in that speech. He does not bind them. He explicitly refuses to use his authority to tie them to a promise they cannot yet be certain of keeping.
