### The Dust Heaps: Boffin's Moral Reckoning (deep_dive)

[Host]
  We've been tracing the dust heaps as an image of wealth accumulated and then redistributed — almost resurrected.  (+600ms @0.98x *redistributed,resurrected*)
  Now I want to turn to the man who inherits all of that dust — and what Dickens does to him morally.  (<800ms> @0.98x *morally*)

[Oliver Trevelyan]
  Noddy Boffin is one of the great comic creations in English fiction — and I say that without any qualification.  (+220ms @1.02x *great,qualification*)

  And yet Dickens puts him through something quite extraordinary — a staged deterioration that is either profound satire or one of the boldest structural gambits in the Victorian novel.  (@0.98x *staged,deterioration,gambits*)
  When Boffin begins his apparent transformation into a miser, obsessively collecting biographies of misers, there is a moment where Dickens gives him this magnificent line.  (@0.97x [setup] *miser,biographies*)
  > "I'll read you the lives of the misers, and you shall hear how they lived and how they died, and then judge for yourself whether a man is to be envied or pitied for having money."  (+150ms @0.93x [reading] *envied,pitied*)

[Edmund Leigh]

### Bella's Golden Cage: Greed and Redemption (deep_dive)

[Host]

### The Masquerade: Harmon's Hidden Identity (close_reading)

[Host]
  We've been tracking Bella's slow liberation from the worship of money — but of course, the novel places her inside a household built entirely on concealment.  (+600ms @0.98x *concealment*)

[Oliver Trevelyan]
  What strikes me first, as a performer, is just how theatrical the whole Harmon construction is.  (+220ms @1.02x *theatrical*)
