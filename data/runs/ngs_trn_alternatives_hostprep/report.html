Daniel Rosen (marxist_critic)
Oliver's right that it's self-diagnosis — but I'd go further. What Reardon is actually exposing is that 'intellectual life' was always already a market — London didn't corrupt it, London just made the mechanism visible. He says — and this is precise — 'we think of London as if it were still the one centre of intellectual life; we think and talk like Chatterton.' Chatterton — the boy who starved in a garret, who invented his own mythology. Gissing is telling us that the romantic idea of the literary life was always a false consciousness that served the market's interests — you convince the talented poor that genius deserves suffering.

Daniel puts it with characteristic force, but I think 'false consciousness' is doing rather a lot of work there. I'd go further than Edmund, actually — Amy's dread isn't just accurate, it's analytical.
