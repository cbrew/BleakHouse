Edmund Leigh (traditionalist_critic)
Daniel, I appreciate the vigour — but I think you're asking the room to carry more freight than Gissing intends. It is simply a room. A rather depressing room, yes — blue sloped walls, school desks, an enormous basket of waste paper. Gissing is a superb naturalist — he puts his characters somewhere real, and then watches what they do. What interests me is not the room — it is what Reardon does with his mind inside it. Because that speech, which Oliver has read with such splendid energy — I hear something else in it. I hear envy dressed up as justice. The rancour Reardon claims to have transcended is the very thing consuming him. He says it would be — and I am quoting now — 'small-minded to think of them with rancour.' The classical invocation is meant to dignify the claim — but it only exposes the self-dramatisation. Gissing is too honest a novelist to let that pass without irony.
