What's at stake is very real, and I think modern readers sometimes miss just how constrained Lucilla's formal options are. And the Rector's scheme is worth dwelling on for a moment, because it wasn't unusual — not at all. A widower's household — particularly a prosperous one — was seen as incomplete, potentially improper, and the parish felt a certain proprietary interest in remedying that. Mr Bury's solution — a respectable widow installed as companion-guardian — was a socially sanctioned move, meant to reassert order. Oliphant renders that intervention with devastating comic fidelity — the Rector speaks of liberty turning unpleasant, of a 'Christian friend to watch over and take care of you.'
