Eleanor Hartley (literary_critic)
It's a brilliant question because what Dickens is doing is something almost cinematic before cinema existed. He builds the threat before the body arrives. By the time we actually see Creakle's face — the fiery face, the small deep-set eyes, the thick veins — we've already been primed to read him as dangerous. But here's what I find extraordinary: Dickens doesn't just use rumour to build dread. He uses it to lock us inside David's child perspective, where you can't distinguish between what's real and what's been amplified by fear. That's sophisticated narrative architecture — and it serves a deeply ethical purpose. We can't step back and judge Creakle coldly because the novel won't let us. We feel what David feels.
