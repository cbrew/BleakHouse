Oliver Trevelyan (performer_and_wit)
The first thing you notice when you read Cranford aloud — and I mean really read it, for the ear — is that Gaskell has almost no waste. Every sentence is doing something — comic, or tender, or both simultaneously. The 'cozy' label is what gets slapped on it by people who've read the first chapter and then stopped. Because actually the novel is a precision instrument — it uses the comedy of small-town propriety to say something devastating about what happens to genteel women when money runs out. The genius is that the devastation arrives wrapped in something that looks like a watercolour sketch of a tea party.
