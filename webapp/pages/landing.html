How it works

Each novel is parsed into passages and enriched with structured metadata. Passages are then selected for discussion by one of three methods:

Transport uses min-cost network flow: each expert declares demand over seven provision dimensions, and the solver finds the cheapest assignment. Zero LLM tokens, under 100ms. Embedding retrieves by cosine similarity and curates with Sonnet — following Anthropic's Contextual Retrieval approach. No passages removes grounding entirely, testing what the LLM knows from memory.

Optionally, host preparation intervenes before script generation:

Pre-interviews surface each expert's key points, desired quotes, and disagreement angles. Question planning synthesises these into targeted host questions. The effect: question density rises from ~1 to ~11 per segment, uniformly across all 15 novels. See the full data in every hostprep report.

Why novels?

The motivation comes from legal and professional information services. RAG is standard, but in practice it is complemented by rich metadata, curated secondary material, and editorial judgement. What happens as this supporting infrastructure becomes less available?

Victorian novels are a safe exploration space: they vary from richly supported (Bleak House, Middlemarch — study guides, Wikipedia, academic papers) to minimally supported (Hester, No Name — almost nothing). By comparing across this spectrum we observe the degradation patterns that matter for any RAG deployment. The research findings show that failure is graduated, vocabulary-preserving, and measurable.
