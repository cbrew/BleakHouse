Method

  • Extract every utterance/turn text body from phase3_episode.json (handles both turn.utterances[].text and turn.text shapes).
  • Normalise typographic apostrophes (’ → ') so don't and don’t share a token. Without this, qwen-plus's preference for curly punctuation inflates s and t as spurious top tokens.
  • Tokenise with [A-Za-z‘’']+, lowercase.
  • For each pair, compute Monroe-Colaresi-Quinn informative-Dirichlet log-odds with α = 0.01, dropping vocab items with combined count < 5. Z-scores are signed: positive favours the first model.
  • A tiny function-word stoplist (~80 items) is applied only for the distinctive-word tables, not for word/type counts or TTR.

Interpretation

Four lexical signatures

  • qwen-plus — contracted, podcast-register, analytical. Heavy contractions plus the most pronounced close-reading lexicon: precisely, architecture, breath, silence, names, grammar, between, reading, and the legal/material vocabulary of the novel (chancery, court, statute, law, fog, verb). Mid-length sentences (12.5 words); among the most lexically diverse.
  • qwen-3-235b — contracted, podcast-register, character-action. Same heavy contractions (he's, she's, it's, isn't, let's, can't, you've, we're, we'll, we've) but the distinctive content vocabulary skews toward characters in motion: look, know, knows, trap, young, died, emotional, power, voice, truth, narrative, jo, narrator, moment. Shortest output (6,520 words); shortest sentences (7.91 words); highest TTR. Reads like a brisker, more dramatic edit of the same episode.
  • gpt-5.4 — essayistic, modal, abstract. First-person studio register (me, think, want, can, almost, listen, make, gives, feels, sounds, inside) and abstract critical vocabulary (social, moral, language, sentence, scene, point, force, pressure, matters). Expands contractions almost universally.
  • sonnet-base — talk-show, reflective, the longest. Same modal register as gpt-5.4, amplified by length: think, want, cannot, something, reading, passage, question, doing, argument, i'd. Disproportionately heavy author/character naming (Dickens, Eleanor).

Two clusters: Qwen-family vs OpenAI/Anthropic

The two contraction-heavy outputs (qwen-plus, qwen-3-235b) form one cluster (Jaccard 0.401); the two essayistic outputs (gpt-5.4, sonnet-base) form a weaker but discernible second cluster (Jaccard 0.386). The dividing line between clusters runs along contractions and register: it's / he's / she's / let's / isn't / can't dominate the Qwen side, while think / want / something / about / cannot dominate the other.

Within the Qwen cluster: analytical vs character-action

qwen-plus and qwen-3-235b agree on register (podcast, spoken, contracted) but differ on what they spend words on. qwen-plus invests in textual mechanics (grammar, verb, syntax, names, architecture, between, reading) and legal-historical specifics (statute, legally, chancery, court). qwen-3-235b invests in characters under pressure (he's young, she's emotional, look, knows, trap, died, voice) and uses fewer textual-mechanics words. Two genuinely different editorial choices on the same source plan.

What this implies for choosing a generator

  • If the desired podcast is close-reading-heavy (panel literally walks through grammar and quotation), qwen-plus's lexical profile is the best fit.
  • If the desired podcast is character-and-stakes-heavy (panel dramatises what happens to Jo, Lady Dedlock, Richard), qwen-3-235b's profile is the best fit.
  • gpt-5.4 and sonnet read more like literary critics talking about the novel than a panel talking through it; for a podcast format, both Qwen options look more on-register.

Production caveats

  • Apostrophes are typographic. qwen-plus emits curly ’; the other three emit straight '. Any downstream code that pattern-matches on apostrophes (TTS pronunciation tweaks, regex accent inference, search indexing) needs a Unicode-normalisation step.
  • Length differences are large. qwen-3-235b at 6,520 words and sonnet at 14,457 words are not comparable on a per-segment basis without normalisation; the audio episodes will differ in runtime by ~2×.
  • Single-novel, single-run signal. One episode per model on one novel; temperature variance not characterised. A repeat draw might shift any of these signatures.
  • Cerebras_qwen metrics gap. Token counts and cost for the qwen-3-235b run weren't archived — that run predates phase3_generation_metrics.json. If we run a fresh qwen-3-235b episode the metrics will be captured.
