Two-paper framing and experiment plan for BleakHouse

This document reframes the experimental agenda around two distinct papers:

Paper 1 (ACL framing)
---------------------
**Question.** How do LLMs behave under grounding and distribution shift?

**Core claim.** Passage grounding largely eliminates hallucinated quotation, while no-grounding
reveals structured failures that depend on likely training exposure.

**What ACL readers should get.**
- Grounding vs no-grounding is the dominant factor for quote faithfulness.
- Confabulation is structured (systematic borrowing), not random noise.
- Prompting alone cannot recover architecture-level effects.
- Effects persist across known vs obscure novels.

Paper 2 (Digital Humanities framing)
------------------------------------
**Question.** Can literary interpretation be made explicit, controllable, and inspectable?

**Core claim.** Interpretation can be operationalized as allocation over textual affordances:
different expert lenses and demand profiles produce distinct, auditable readings.

**What DH readers should get.**
- Passage selection is itself an interpretive act.
- Lens parameters (demand vectors) are manipulable and measurable.
- Textual affordances vary across novels and reshape expert prominence.
- The pipeline scaffolds meaningful discussion even for less-canonical works.

Shared experiment matrix
------------------------
Each experiment should explicitly say which paper(s) it supports.

1) Grounding dominance benchmark (ACL)
- Conditions: transport, embedding, random, plain-RAG, no-passages.
- Metrics: quote verification, confabulation rate.
- Decision rule: support if grounded >= 90% verification and no-passages <= 50% average.

2) Novel-dependent ignorance test (ACL)
- Conditions: no-passages runs across familiar vs less-familiar novels.
- Metrics: verification by novel; rank correlation with exposure proxies.
- Decision rule: support if familiar novels significantly outperform obscure novels.

3) Cross-corpus confabulation mechanism (ACL, DH)
- Conditions: take quotes from obscure no-passages episodes; compare against:
  (a) all in-project novel corpora, and (b) broader 19th-century reference corpora.
- Metrics:
  - exact/near-exact overlap rate with any single known novel (expected to be rare),
  - semantic nearest-neighbor author attribution,
  - stylistic attribution confidence (author/period classifier),
  - generic-Victorian prototype score (high lexical overlap with broad-era language, low overlap
    with any specific source novel).
- Decision rule:
  - strong version: repeated high-confidence attribution to a specific known author/text family;
  - weaker but still useful version: confabulations look "period-plausible" yet do not map to
    specific source passages, indicating prototype-level borrowing rather than direct quotation.

4) Architecture vs prompt factorial (ACL)
- Conditions: shared passages ON/OFF x cross-engagement instruction ON/OFF.
- Metrics: cross-reference rate, turn-transition graph density.
- Decision rule: support if shared-context effect size is clearly larger than instruction effect.

5) Host-prep interaction under ignorance (ACL, DH)
- Conditions: host-prep ON/OFF for familiar and obscure novels, transport fixed.
- Metrics: questions per segment, reactive markers, expert vocabulary stability.
- Decision rule: support if host-prep lift is larger on obscure novels with limited identity drift.

6) Interpretive controllability benchmark (DH)
- Conditions: baseline vs peaked vs arc-boosted demand vectors.
- Metrics: passage/chapter Jaccard, solver latency, token cost.
- Decision rule: support if editorial changes produce predictable passage shifts at near-zero marginal tokens.

7) Schema transfer and bias stress test (DH)
- Conditions: run enrichment on existing + candidate novels using same seven dimensions.
- Metrics: per-dimension strong-rate; detect empty/saturated dimensions.
- Decision rule: support transfer if no dimension collapses; support bias if specific dimensions collapse on non-Dickensian texts.

8) Prompt-pruning ablations (ACL)
- Conditions: remove one constraint at a time (quote pattern, prosody, sentence-type, timing).
- Metrics: structure compliance, prosodic variance, blind preference.
- Decision rule: keep only constraints with measurable output benefit.

Interpretation note on obscure-novel confabulation
--------------------------------------------------
For obscure novels, we should treat direct recoverable quotes from Dickens/Eliot as a possible
outcome, not the default expectation. A more realistic baseline is:
- low exact overlap with specific novels,
- but detectable pull toward high-frequency Victorian diction and narrative templates.

So the experiment should be powered to detect both:
1) specific-source borrowing (rare but high-signal when present), and
2) diffuse prototype borrowing (likely, and still theoretically important).

Execution order
---------------
1. Grounding dominance benchmark (fastest high-value ACL result).
2. Architecture vs prompt factorial.
3. Prompt-pruning ablations (to reduce cost/noise for downstream runs).
4. Interpretive controllability benchmark.
5. Novel-dependent ignorance + host-prep interaction (run together on shared artifacts).
6. Cross-novel confabulation mechanism.
7. Schema transfer/bias stress test.

Reporting template (for every experiment)
-----------------------------------------
- Hypothesis ID(s)
- Paper relevance: ACL / DH / both
- Run commands and fixed variables
- Metrics and decision thresholds
- Result: confirmed / disconfirmed / inconclusive
- Implications for paper claims
- Next follow-up run
Here are concise, high-signal summaries of the two papers as they would be understood by their respective audiences.

📄 Paper 1 (ACL framing)
Core idea

A controlled experimental study of how grounding, prior knowledge, and system architecture shape LLM behavior in multi-agent text generation.

Key claim

Providing explicit textual grounding largely eliminates hallucination, while lack of grounding reveals structured, distribution-dependent failure modes tied to the model’s training exposure.

What’s new
Grounding vs no grounding is the dominant factor in quote fidelity, not retrieval method.
Hallucination is not random: it systematically borrows from better-known training data.
LLM “knowledge” is measurable and instance-dependent (varies by text).
Shared context (architecture) induces interaction more reliably than prompt instructions.
Why ACL cares
Advances understanding of:
hallucination mechanisms
distribution shift
RAG vs architectural design
Provides clean ablations and generalizable insights beyond the literary domain.
One-line takeaway

LLM reliability in generation tasks is primarily a function of grounding and training exposure, not retrieval sophistication or prompt design.

📄 Paper 2 (Digital Humanities framing)
Core idea

A computational framework that models literary interpretation as a structured, controllable process, linking textual features to interpretive perspectives and resulting discourse.

Key claim

Literary interpretation can be operationalized as an allocation problem over textual affordances, where different critical lenses produce distinct, inspectable readings of the same text.

What’s new
Introduces explicit, parameterized interpretive lenses (e.g., Marxist, formalist).
Defines textual affordances (provision dimensions) that vary across works.
Shows that:
different lenses select different passages
texts afford different kinds of readings
Reveals that:
misquotation becomes intertextual contamination when knowledge is weak
scholarly discourse can be scaffolded without prior expertise
Why DH cares
Makes interpretation:
explicit
manipulable
empirically analyzable
Bridges:
close reading
computational methods
theories of interpretation
One-line takeaway

Literary interpretation can be formalized as a controllable mapping between textual affordances and critical perspectives, producing reproducible and analyzable readings.

🧭 The difference in one sentence

ACL paper:

How LLMs behave under grounding and distribution shift.

DH paper:

How interpretation itself can be modeled, controlled, and studied computationally.
